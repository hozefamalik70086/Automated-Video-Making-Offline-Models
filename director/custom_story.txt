Shot List (24 clips × 5 seconds each = 2 minutes)
Clip 1
Wide shot. The red umbrella leans against a lonely park bench. A gust of wind ruffles the canopy. The first raindrops spot the fabric.

Clip 2
Close-up. The umbrella’s handle rattles against the bench slats. Suddenly the wind catches it—the umbrella lifts and rolls out of frame.

Clip 3
Low angle, tracking with the umbrella. The umbrella tumbles and spins across a carpet of wet orange leaves, its red a vivid slash against the grey.

Clip 4
Medium shot. A toddler reaches out a chubby hand as the umbrella cartwheels past. Fingertips graze the fabric but miss. The child’s face shows wonder, not sadness.

Clip 5
Upward tilt. The umbrella snags on the branch of a bare tree. It flaps wildly for a moment, like a trapped bird, the city skyline blurred behind it.

Clip 6
Sidewalk level. A jogger’s bright trainers splash through a puddle, just centimetres from the umbrella now lying open on the ground. The runner doesn’t break stride.

Clip 7
Macro shot. The wind releases the umbrella from the branch. It drops into frame and skids across wet tarmac, metal tips scraping, coming to rest half-open.

Clip 8
Overhead. A businessman in a sharp suit steps neatly over the umbrella, phone pressed to his ear, his polished shoe barely missing the red fabric.

Clip 9
Slow motion. Rain begins to fall harder. Droplets bounce and slide across the taut red nylon, each one glistening before it rolls off the edge.

Clip 10
Medium shot. A stray dog sniffs the umbrella’s handle, tail wagging once. It sneezes, then trots away, its nails clicking on the pavement.

Clip 11
Street corner. A street musician with a worn guitar case sees the rain. He picks up the red umbrella, opens it fully, and props it over his guitar. He plays three soft chords and smiles.

Clip 12
Static wide. The musician finishes his song, carefully closes the umbrella, and leans it against a graffiti-covered wall. He walks away, leaving it behind again.

Clip 13
Reflective puddle shot. The umbrella lies beside a puddle that mirrors flickering neon signs and passing headlights. The red canopy shimmers on the water’s surface.

Clip 14
Eye-level. A little girl in a bright yellow raincoat and polka-dot boots spots the umbrella. She walks over, kneels, and picks it up, her face lighting up with a gap-toothed smile.

Clip 15
Slow-motion spin. The girl twirls the open umbrella above her head. Water droplets fly off in a sparkling arc. Her laughter is silent but palpable.

Clip 16
Tracking shot, behind. The girl walks hand-in-hand with her mother. The red umbrella bobs above them, a moving spot of colour against the grey city.

Clip 17
Through a bakery window. The pair pauses outside. Condensation fogs the glass. The girl presses her nose to the window, the umbrella tilting, while warm bread glows inside.

Clip 18
Subway entrance. They descend stairs. The umbrella drips a trail of water onto the steps. The mother folds it and hands it to the girl, who hugs it like a doll.

Clip 19
Train interior. The girl sits on a flip-down seat, the closed red umbrella across her lap. She traces patterns on the wet fabric with her finger. The train rocks gently.

Clip 20
Station platform. They exit the train. The girl insists on carrying the umbrella herself, dragging its tip slightly along the ground, making a thin line on the concrete.

Clip 21
Emerging into a night market. Lanterns hang overhead. The girl holds the umbrella high like a torch. Steam rises from food stalls. Everything feels warm and golden.

Clip 22
Quiet porch. An elderly woman in a rocking chair sits under a bare bulb. The girl runs to her, proudly presenting the red umbrella. The grandmother’s wrinkled hands receive it.

Clip 23
Two-shot, from behind. The grandmother opens the umbrella and holds it over both of them on the porch, even though there’s no rain now. They sit together, looking at the stars.

Clip 24
Final close-up. The red umbrella rests against the rocking chair, indoors now. A small, brightly patterned patch—clearly sewn by a child—covers a tiny tear. A ray of morning sunlight warms the fabric. Hold.
